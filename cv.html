Research Interests

Differential topology and mathematical physics.

I work at the interface of geometry, topology, and physics, especially gauge theory on principal bundles, holonomy, and higher parallel transport in the framework of generalized smooth spaces such as diffeological spaces. My current work develops a diffeological approach to connections and holonomy on path spaces. Broader interests include deformation quantization and the geometric foundations of classical and quantum mechanics.

MATH 1070Q Mathematics for Business and Economics Summer 2026

CAPS/SSS Summer Bridge Program · designed the entire course

  • Five-week intensive course with daily 110-minute classes, part of UConn’s summer bridge program for first-generation, low-income, and underrepresented students.
  • Designed every component: syllabus, LaTeX lecture slides, daily worksheets, homework sets, quizzes, exams, and a course webpage. Slides and homework are collected on the Instructor Resources page.
  • Built the course around Second Chance Grading (O. E. Fernandez, PRIMUS, 2021): each topic is assessed by a quiz and again on a weekly “second chance” exam, and the higher score counts.
MATH 1020Q Problem Solving Spring 2025 – Spring 2026

Two lecture sections per semester

  • Paired adapted lecture slides with live whiteboard work: slides pose questions that are then solved with the class in real time.
  • Designed a six-question quiz format in which only four questions count and one is always a vocabulary question, rewarding attention to mathematical language and lowering the stakes of any single hard problem.
  • Student evaluations (≈ 90 respondents): mean overall instructor rating 4.7/5, compared with a department mean of 3.8–3.9 for 1000-level courses.
