Research

My research is in differential topology and mathematical physics. I am primarily studying gauge theory, that is, the theory of connections on principal bundles and the holonomy they determine. I am particularly interested in connections over objects with path-like data, and in carrying gauge-theoretic constructions over to infinite-dimensional spaces such as path spaces, where they connect to higher gauge theory. In these settings I work in the framework of diffeology, Souriau’s theory of generalized smooth spaces, which give these spaces of interest a natural smooth structure.

In my recent paper, I use diffeology to give a rigorous construction of I. M. Singer’s universal connection on a bundle of paths over a manifold. Extending this connection to the diffeological setting, I show that a diffeological principal bundle with connection can be reconstructed from its holonomy, yielding an equivalence between a category of holonomy data and the category of diffeological principal bundles with connection. My (ongoing) dissertation continues this program, developing a diffeological approach to connections and holonomy on path spaces.

More broadly, I am interested in the geometric foundations of classical and quantum mechanics, including symplectic and Poisson geometry and deformation quantization, the subject of my undergraduate honors thesis. Overall, my work draws on differential geometry and topology, Lie theory, and category theory.
